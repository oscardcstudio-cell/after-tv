How After TV works

You play a track. Your browser listens and cuts Rien's film library to it, beat by beat.

No server does the work, and nobody stands at the decks. The ear is a JavaScript library. The brain and the edit are the Python code Rien plays its own live sets with, running inside this page: one program for both (V48).

Each dot crossing the chain is one beat of the track playing now. Use the arrow keys to move along it.

VJing

VJing means projecting a silent film whose soundtrack is the DJ's music. The VJ sorts hundreds of pieces of a puzzle by theme, then movement, then rhythm, and live, guesses what the DJ plays next.

Cinema, not graphics

Rien does not loop artificial graphics that always look the same. Rien plays cinema and the living, from abstract to amateur to 3D, sampled the way electronic music samples records.

The library

1,137 videos, about 12 hours, cut shot by shot and put online in 12 volumes, so the next generations don't have to squeeze every film themselves.

01

Sound

The sound comes in

Three ways in. On desktop Chrome or Edge, you share a screen with its audio: After TV throws away the picture and keeps the sound. Anywhere else, it plays Intergalactic FM (an internet radio with three channels) or listens through the microphone, which hears the room and is less precise.

Sound under -60 dB in the mids and highs counts as silence. After 4 seconds of it, the screen goes black with AFTER TV in the middle. The film returns with the sound (V74).

02

Ear

The ear

The ear is live-audio-analyzer, an open-source library by hssnadr, loaded from its repository at a fixed version. Every 256 samples, it runs a 1024-point FFT on the sound.

From the attacks it finds the tempo and the first beat of each bar. A separate detector catches the kick drum. Every 5 seconds, YAMNet, Google's sound classifier, names the genre.

The genre steers the edit only when the visitor picks Auto. With a style chosen (House by default), the genre is shown here and does nothing else.

03

Ear

The groove of the bar

Every kick, snare and hi-hat hit lands on one of the bar's 16 steps. Read together, the three lines get a name: four-on-the-floor, broken beat, light kick or no kick.

When the name changes, the edit swaps its dominant loop at the end of that bar (V71), at most once every 8 bars.

The ear also catches a sudden silence, the stop before a drop. When After TV follows Rien's dashboard, it cuts to black, and the first kick brings the image back.

04

Brain

Where we are in the track

The brain counts bars, groups them into 8-bar phrases, and names the part it hears: intro, verse, build-up, chorus, break or outro, with its confidence.

It also notices when a part or a riff comes back, and can announce a drop a few bars ahead. The edit uses all three. A returning part or riff brings back the loops it had the first time. An announced drop gets its loops ready one bar early.

The part also sets how many loops share the screen, and how long the loops around the dominant one stay.

05

Brain

Four modes

Beneath the parts, the edit plays one of four modes: the four buttons on Rien's controller.

Every mode change, and every tool the autopilot touches, goes into a journal with its reason. The latest entries show here.

06

Brain

The texture of the sound

Beyond rhythm, the ear describes the material of the sound: bright or dull, smooth or rough, dry or reverberant, squashed or airy, with or without a voice or an instrument.

Each word points to visual tags. A dull sound asks for low-key images, a rough one for hard lines and square shapes. These tags break ties: among loops that fit the edit equally well, the ones that match the sound win.

The ear measures more than the edit uses so far. What is measured but not wired is listed at the bottom.

07

Edit

The library

Every loop comes from Rien's Resolume composition: twelve volumes of film, cut shot by shot.

The composition is sorted in chapters along its timeline, so neighboring columns tend to belong together. Each loop carries a card of tags: speed, the modes it suits, house or techno, and a visual block (tone, color, lines, movement, shape, space, framing, subject).

08

Edit

Choosing the next loop

When a loop leaves, its replacement is drawn next to the loop that stays. First, the pool: loops that fit the mood, are not on screen, and have not played recently (V21).

Then the link. A candidate shares at least one tag with the loop that stays (V46), and the draw follows Rien's own edits.

Now and then, a pair that Rien cut by hand comes back.

The candidates shown here come from running the real draw (montage.js, the browser copy of the one in live.py) on the real catalog, starting from the loop on screen now.

09

Edit

Cutting on the beat

Cuts land on the beat. Each sequence of a drop opens on the kick, moves to the snare, then ends on the hi-hat. A fast edit runs 8-bar sequences, a slower one 16 (V98).

Around the dominant loop, one loop changes every 2 to 4 bars depending on the part (V41), and never more than one at a time: the pair x y becomes x z (V42). The edit counts in bars, never in seconds, so it breathes with the tempo; a mask laid on top holds 16 bars (V90). Inside a loop, cue points let the edit jump to another moment of the same shot.

10

Screen

The screen

Resolume, rebuilt in the page: up to three layers, stacked as in Arena. Each layer holds two videos. The next loop loads in the hidden one and comes to the front only when a frame is ready at the chosen cue, so the screen never flashes black.

Alpha layers swap on the beat. 50 Add lays a layer's light over what is under it. In CSS, that is mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter.

The videos are H.264 with a keyframe every 12 frames, so jumping to a cue point is instant. They are served from Cloudflare R2 for about $0 to $1 a month.

Rules

Where it comes from

People kept asking how to get these images. After TV is the answer: a program that edits the archive live, on any music, in a website, for any TV, any projector, a bar or a beginner DJ.

The 2024 dossier imagined an AI trained by watching Rien play for hours. We wrote rules instead, each one after Rien watched the edit and corrected it (step 11).

Cinema's craft of framing and sequence shots guides the choice of films. Two rules decide which shots qualify (V91, V92).
